The problem

In Karamoja, farming still waits for a rain that increasingly does not come.

Households in Abim plant against two short, uncertain windows a year. When the rains fail or arrive late, a season is lost — and with it the food and the income it would have carried.

Irrigation is the obvious answer and the rarest one. Surface and flood methods, the ones most within reach, lose roughly four of every ten buckets to runoff, evaporation and drainage below the root. In a place where water is scarce and often carried by hand, that loss is the difference between a method that works and one that doesn't.

Young people leave because farming no longer pays. That is the gap this project is built in.

Approach

Put the water at the root, and run it on the sun.

Precision drip irrigation delivers water slowly, directly at the base of each plant, through emitters on lines running down every crop row. The soil between rows stays dry. Almost nothing is lost.

Solar panels drive the pump, so the system has no fuel cost and produces most when the sun is strongest — which is when crops need water most.

The gain is not marginal. It is the difference between delivering six buckets in ten and delivering nine.

The pilot

A demonstration we run ourselves, at our own cost and risk.

The system was built and tested as a prototype at the Makerere University botanical garden. It worked there. The question this pilot answers is whether it works in Abim soil, in Abim heat, on Abim water.

Oasis Centauri installs and operates the demonstration plot. Nothing about it is handed over during the pilot — not the land, the equipment or the systems. What transfers to the community is the skill, taught on a working installation rather than in a classroom.

Youth inclusion

Training first. Ownership later, and only if it is earned by the evidence.

Young people aged 18 to 30 from the host villages are trained on the system and then work on it hands-on, under supervision. The skill is theirs permanently, whatever happens to the pilot.

Those who wish to go further sign a founding members' register for a cooperative that may be formed once the pilot closes in 2028. Nothing about that cooperative is settled now — its leadership, member contributions and sharing of benefits will be decided by by-laws its own members write at formation.

We say this plainly in every village meeting, because a project that overpromises in its first season has nothing left to offer in its third.

Who we are

A student project that went to the field.

Oasis Centauri was founded by students of Makerere University, where the precision drip system was first built and tested. It is now implementing that prototype as a funded pilot in Karamoja.
